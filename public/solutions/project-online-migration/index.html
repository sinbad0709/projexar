Why every route out seems to start with a phone call

The organisations answering this question are mostly Microsoft partners and specialist PPM consultancies, and their business model is a discovery phase followed by a statement of work. That's the right answer for a large PMO with years of portfolio history to preserve. If your position is a team, a set of live projects and a resourcing problem that just lost its tool, it's a lot of process to reach a decision you could make in a week.

Point ProjexaR at your files.

ProjexaR reads .mpp files directly from a folder — no template to populate, no manual re-entry. It also reads Excel, and Planner plans exported to Excel. What comes across:

  • Projects with their dates, status and lifecycle phase
  • Tasks and durations
  • Workstreams
  • Predecessor and successor links
  • Resources and their assignments
  • Allocation percentages
  • Working patterns

What it can't infer, it tells you: an exception report lists missing line managers, missing email addresses, incomplete project fields, duplicate records and any allocation that already exceeds 100%. You resolve those before you go live rather than discovering them afterwards. Email addresses are the predictable failure — .mpp files rarely carry them even when everything else maps cleanly.

What doesn't come across.

  • Enterprise custom fields and custom field structures
  • Portfolio analysis and prioritisation models
  • Project Server workflow and approval configuration
  • Anything built on OData feeds or the reporting database, including Power BI reports fed by them
  • Historical baselines beyond what's in the files themselves
  • Start-to-start and finish-to-finish dependencies — v1 supports finish-to-start only, so tasks import but those relationships don't
If your reporting depends on OData, ProjexaR has no API in this version. Better you know that now than sixty days in.

Four situations ProjexaR was built for.

Each of these is a real planning problem in an IT team. If one of them is yours, the Capacity Check will show you the shape of it in a few minutes.

01

You're leaving Project Online

Project Online closes on 30 September 2026 and there's no first-party migration path. Project Plan 5 — the only Microsoft SKU with resource capacity planning — went end of sale in May. Most of the answers to this involve a discovery phase and a statement of work.

See the migration route
02

The plan lives in a spreadsheet

Someone owns the resourcing sheet. It's accurate the day it's built and drifting by the second week, and when a project moves, updating it is an evening's work that nobody has. The problem isn't the spreadsheet — it's that a change in one project has consequences the sheet can't calculate for you.

03

BAU keeps eating the projects

Your team supports the business and delivers change from the same pool of resources. BAU — business as usual — wins, because it's louder. Projects slip and nobody can say exactly why; the hours were never really there. ProjexaR makes that time explicit: the line manager declares it, and project plans are checked against what's genuinely left.

04

You're asked whether you can take something on

A new request arrives with a date attached. Answering honestly means knowing what your team's next three months already look like — including work that hasn't started. ProjexaR lets you test a proposed project against forecast capacity before you commit to it, so the answer is evidence rather than instinct.

What this doesn't do

ProjexaR plans capacity against projects. It isn't a service desk, a time-tracking system or a billing platform, and it doesn't replace your ticketing tool. If you need utilisation reporting for client invoicing, this isn't the product.
